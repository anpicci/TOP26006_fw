# Roles and authority

The human researcher or scientific owner chooses the research purpose, method and interpretation boundary, authorizes exact effects within their authority, and makes final acceptance decisions for the relevant project or scientific claim. Those decisions are recorded with evidence and limits; a tool result cannot make them implicitly.

A coordinating Chat or another authorized prompt author translates the owner's mandate into an execution contract. It reads live [CPC.md](../../CPC.md), [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), registry/module and native policy, and accepted prior evidence; resolves or gates branch applicability; sets exact scope, permissions, validation, evidence and stop conditions; and adversarially rereads the prompt. A knowledgeable human can [author manually](../how_to/write_a_prompt_manually.md), but human prompt authorship is an alternative, not a mandatory role.

The executor rereads required live instructions, verifies volatile preconditions, performs only authorized work, stops on contradictions or new boundaries, preserves evidence, and returns observations and claims. Executor completion is not acceptance.

A separately tasked reviewer or coordinating Chat compares the mandate and exact prompt with returned evidence, reproduces decision-material checks where feasible, classifies blocking/nonblocking findings, preserves valid work, and recommends acceptance, repair, or stop. Same-executor self-review is not independent review. AI adversarial review is not human peer review or independent scientific replication merely because it challenges a claim. The human owner's acceptance remains a separate decision.

When evidence crosses repositories, each native owner and mutation boundary remains separate. Qualified analysis evidence may inform a methods record but grants no methods access. [Sequential coordination](multi_repository_research.md) and [evidence states](../reference/evidence_and_task_states.md) explain these boundaries.
