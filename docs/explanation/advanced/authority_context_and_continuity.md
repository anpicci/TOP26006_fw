# Authority, context, and continuity

The scientific owner decides whether evidence supports a scientific claim. A responsible researcher or workstream role owns a bounded question and its follow-through. A coordinator tracks dependencies, conflicts, and handoffs, but has no automatic scientific veto. A prompt author turns an authorized task into an explicit contract. An executor performs that bounded task. A reviewer checks the exact evidence. A receiver takes over under an effective owner-authorized transfer, with the cited evidence acquired and understood before new work relies on the handoff.

These roles can be held by the same person in a small project, but the decisions remain distinct. Result execution, delivery, review, acceptance, and downstream readiness each need their own evidence. A coordinator's record documents project decisions; it cannot replace the owner or existing governance.

A conversation or session can end, be replaced, or lose context while responsibility and external effects continue. A transcript can help reconstruct what was said, but it does not prove scientific provenance or stop a running command. A handoff states the last verified state, unresolved effects, accepted evidence, and next permitted action. The [synthetic transfer](../../examples/advanced_parallel_workstreams/coordination.md) records sender preparation, receiver acknowledgment of receipt and scope, a separate bounded receiver readback of cited evidence and continuation boundary, and the effective authority reference. Receipt and acknowledgment alone do not prove comprehension; authority transfer is a separate owner decision. Changing the executor or session alone establishes none of these facts.
